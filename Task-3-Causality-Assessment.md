# Introduction

Causality assessment is an important component of Pharmacovigilance that helps evaluate the likelihood of a relationship between a medicinal product and a reported adverse event.

The assessment considers multiple factors rather than assuming that an adverse event occurring after administration of a drug was necessarily caused by that drug.

---

# Key Factors Considered in Causality Assessment

Important factors that may be evaluated include:

- Temporal relationship between drug exposure and the adverse event
- Clinical characteristics of the reaction
- Response to withdrawal of the suspected drug (dechallenge)
- Response to re-administration of the drug (rechallenge), if available
- Alternative causes for the event
- Concomitant medications
- Relevant medical history
- Objective clinical or laboratory evidence
- Previous knowledge or reports of the suspected reaction

---

# Dechallenge

**Dechallenge** refers to what happens to an adverse event after the suspected medicinal product is withdrawn or its dose is reduced.

### Positive Dechallenge
The reaction improves or disappears after withdrawal of the suspected drug.

### Negative Dechallenge
The reaction does not improve after withdrawal of the suspected drug.

Dechallenge information can contribute to the overall assessment of the drug–event relationship.

---

# Rechallenge

**Rechallenge** refers to what happens when the suspected medicinal product is administered again after it has previously been withdrawn.

### Positive Rechallenge
The adverse reaction reappears following re-administration of the suspected drug.

### Negative Rechallenge
The adverse reaction does not reappear following re-administration.

Rechallenge information may not always be available and intentional rechallenge may be inappropriate or unsafe in some clinical situations.

---

# Causality Assessment Methods

## 1. WHO-UMC Causality Assessment System

The WHO-UMC system provides standardized categories for evaluating the relationship between a medicinal product and an adverse event.

The major categories include:

- **Certain**
- **Probable / Likely**
- **Possible**
- **Unlikely**
- **Conditional / Unclassified**
- **Unassessable / Unclassifiable**

The final category depends on the quality and completeness of the available case information.

---

## 2. Naranjo Algorithm

The **Naranjo Adverse Drug Reaction Probability Scale** uses a structured questionnaire to assess the likelihood that an adverse reaction is related to a particular drug.

The questions consider factors such as:

- Previous reports of the reaction
- Timing of the event after drug administration
- Improvement following drug withdrawal
- Reappearance following rechallenge
- Alternative causes
- Dose-response relationship
- Objective evidence

The responses are assigned scores, and the total score is used to categorize the relationship.

### General Interpretation

| Total Score | Causality Category |
|---|---|
| ≥ 9 | Definite |
| 5–8 | Probable |
| 1–4 | Possible |
| ≤ 0 | Doubtful |
