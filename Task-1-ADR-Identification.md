## 1. What is an Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR)?

An **Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR)** is a harmful and unintended response to a medicinal product occurring at doses normally used for prevention, diagnosis, or treatment of disease.

ADR identification involves reviewing the patient's symptoms, medication history, relevant medical conditions, and the timeline between drug exposure and the onset of the reaction.

---

## 2. Classification of ADRs

ADR classifications covered in the training include:

- **Pharmacological ADRs:** Related to the known pharmacological effects of a drug.
- **Pathological ADRs:** Associated with pathological changes or disease-related effects.
- **Genotoxic ADRs:** Related to damage or alteration of genetic material.
- **Allergic ADRs:** Immune-mediated reactions occurring after exposure to a drug.
- **Idiosyncratic ADRs:** Unusual and unpredictable reactions that are not related to the usual pharmacological action of the drug.
- **Pharmacogenetic ADRs:** Reactions influenced by genetic differences between individuals.
- **Teratogenic effects:** Drug-related effects that may cause developmental abnormalities in an embryo or fetus.

---

## 3. Hypersensitivity Reactions

Drug-related hypersensitivity reactions can be classified into four major types:

1. **Type I – Immediate hypersensitivity:** Usually IgE-mediated and may result in reactions such as urticaria or anaphylaxis.
2. **Type II – Cytotoxic reaction:** Antibody-mediated reaction involving cell or tissue damage.
3. **Type III – Immune-complex reaction:** Caused by deposition of antigen-antibody complexes.
4. **Type IV – Delayed-type hypersensitivity:** T-cell-mediated reaction that generally develops after a delay.

Understanding these mechanisms helps in recognizing and documenting suspected immune-mediated ADRs.

## 4. Teratogenic Drug Categories

The training also covered the **historical FDA pregnancy risk categories**:

- **Category A:** Controlled studies did not demonstrate a risk to the fetus.
- **Category B:** Animal studies did not demonstrate fetal risk, although adequate human studies were limited.
- **Category C:** Risk could not be ruled out; potential benefits may justify use in certain situations.
- **Category D:** Evidence of fetal risk exists, but benefits may sometimes justify use.
- **Category X:** Evidence of fetal abnormalities or risk; potential risks outweigh any possible benefit.

> **Note:** The FDA replaced the A, B, C, D and X letter categories with the Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule (PLLR) in 2015. The letter categories are therefore considered historical.

## 5. Causality Assessment

Causality assessment helps evaluate whether a suspected adverse event may be related to a particular medicinal product.

### Bradford Hill Criteria

A set of considerations used to evaluate whether an observed association may represent a causal relationship. Factors include temporality, biological plausibility, consistency, strength of association, and other considerations.

### WHO-UMC Causality Assessment

The WHO-UMC system categorizes suspected drug-event relationships into categories such as:

- Certain
- Probable/Likely
- Possible
- Unlikely
- Conditional/Unclassified
- Unassessable/Unclassifiable

### Naranjo Algorithm

A structured questionnaire-based approach used to estimate the probability that an adverse event is related to a drug. The resulting score helps categorize the relationship as **doubtful, possible, probable, or definite**.

## 6. ADR Reporting in India

ADR reporting is an important component of pharmacovigilance because it helps identify previously unrecognized safety signals and contributes to the continuous monitoring of medicines.

During the training, I learned about reporting suspected ADRs through:

- **CDSCO ADR reporting forms**
- **Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI)**
- **PvPI mobile application**

Important information in an ADR report may include patient details, suspected medicine(s), description of the reaction, relevant dates, concomitant medications, and reporter information.

---

## 7. Pharmacovigilance and Public Health

**Pharmacovigilance** is the science and activities concerned with the detection, assessment, understanding, and prevention of adverse effects or other medicine-related problems.

Its major role is to improve patient safety by continuously monitoring the benefit-risk profile of medicines.

### Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI)

PvPI is India's national pharmacovigilance programme, established to monitor the safety of medicines and collect suspected ADR reports from healthcare professionals and other reporting sources.

---

## 8. Practical ADR Identification Approach

When reviewing a suspected ADR case, the following steps can be considered:

1. Review the patient's presenting signs and symptoms.
2. Review the medication history.
3. Identify the suspected drug and dose.
4. Establish the timeline between drug exposure and onset of the reaction.
5. Consider alternative causes and relevant medical conditions.
6. Assess the possibility of a drug-event relationship.
7. Document the suspected ADR accurately.
8. Report the ADR through the appropriate pharmacovigilance reporting system.
