## Task Objective

The objective of this task is to understand the complete **Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR) case-processing workflow** followed in Pharmacovigilance and Drug Safety activities.

This task focuses on the systematic processing of an individual safety case, from initial receipt of a safety report through data entry, assessment, quality review, follow-up, and case closure.

---

# 1. What is an ICSR?

An **Individual Case Safety Report (ICSR)** is a report containing information about one or more suspected adverse events associated with the use of a medicinal product in an individual patient.

ICSRs are an important component of Pharmacovigilance because they provide information that can be evaluated for potential safety signals and help in monitoring the safety profile of medicines.

---

# 2. ICSR Case Processing Workflow

The major steps involved in ICSR case processing are:

1. Case Receipt
2. Case Identification and Triage
3. Validity Assessment
4. Duplicate Check
5. Data Entry
6. Medical Coding
7. Case Assessment
8. Seriousness Assessment
9. Expectedness Assessment
10. Causality/Relatedness Assessment
11. Narrative Writing
12. Quality Control (QC)
13. Follow-up
14. Case Submission
15. Case Closure and Archiving

---

## Step 1 – Case Receipt

The first step is receiving a safety report from an available source.

### Possible Sources

- Healthcare professionals
- Patients or consumers
- Regulatory authorities
- Clinical trials
- Literature
- Pharmaceutical companies
- Market research programs
- Patient support programs
- Partners or third-party organizations

The received information is reviewed to determine whether it may represent a potential safety case.

---

## Step 2 – Case Identification and Triage

The incoming report is reviewed and prioritized.

During triage, the available information is examined to identify:

- Patient information
- Suspected medicinal product
- Adverse event/reaction
- Reporter information
- Source of the report
- Date of receipt
- Seriousness
- Any important medical information

Cases may be prioritized based on seriousness, regulatory timelines, and other safety criteria.

---

## Step 3 – Validity Assessment

Before processing an ICSR, the case should contain the minimum information required for a valid safety report.

### Four Minimum Criteria for a Valid ICSR

1. **Identifiable patient**
2. **Identifiable reporter**
3. **Suspected medicinal product**
4. **Suspected adverse event/reaction**

If one or more required elements are missing, appropriate follow-up may be required to obtain additional information.

---

## Step 4 – Duplicate Check

A duplicate search is performed to determine whether the same case has already been received.

Information that may be compared includes:

- Patient characteristics
- Reporter details
- Suspected drug
- Adverse event
- Event onset date
- Medical history
- Case source
- Other unique case information

Duplicate checking helps prevent the same safety case from being processed multiple times.

---

# 5. Data Entry

Once the case is considered valid, the available information is entered into the Pharmacovigilance safety database.

### Important Data Elements

#### Patient Information

- Patient initials or identifier
- Age
- Sex
- Weight
- Relevant medical history

#### Suspected Product Information

- Generic/brand name
- Dose
- Route of administration
- Frequency
- Indication
- Therapy dates
- Batch/lot number, when available

#### Adverse Event Information

- Event/reaction term
- Date of onset
- Outcome
- Relevant clinical information
- Laboratory findings, when available

#### Reporter Information

- Reporter type
- Reporter qualification
- Contact information, where permitted

---

# 6. Medical Coding

Medical coding converts reported information into standardized terminology.

### Common Coding Areas

- Adverse events/reactions
- Medical history
- Indications
- Medicinal products

For adverse events, **MedDRA (Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities)** is commonly used for standardized coding.

The reported verbatim term should be reviewed carefully before selecting the appropriate MedDRA term.

---

# 7. Seriousness Assessment

The case is assessed to determine whether the adverse event meets regulatory criteria for a **serious adverse event/reaction**.

Seriousness criteria include:

- Death
- Life-threatening condition
- Hospitalization or prolongation of hospitalization
- Persistent or significant disability/incapacity
- Congenital anomaly/birth defect
- Other medically important condition

> **Important:** Seriousness is not the same as severity.

### Seriousness

Determined using regulatory seriousness criteria.

### Severity

Describes the intensity of the event, such as:

- Mild
- Moderate
- Severe

---

# 8. Expectedness Assessment

The reported reaction is assessed against the applicable reference safety information.

The assessment determines whether the event is:

- Expected
- Unexpected

The applicable reference document may depend on the type of product and reporting context.

Examples may include:

- Company Core Safety Information
- Investigator's Brochure
- Approved product information
- Local prescribing information

---

# 9. Causality / Relatedness Assessment

Causality assessment evaluates the relationship between the suspected medicinal product and the reported adverse event.

Factors that may be considered include:

- Temporal relationship
- Dechallenge
- Rechallenge
- Alternative explanations
- Concomitant medications
- Medical history
- Known pharmacological effects
- Biological plausibility
- Previous knowledge of the reaction

Common causality approaches include:

- WHO-UMC system
- Naranjo algorithm

The final assessment should be based on the available clinical information and applicable procedures.

---

# 10. Dechallenge and Rechallenge

### Dechallenge

Dechallenge refers to the outcome of an adverse event after withdrawal, interruption, or reduction of the suspected medicinal product.

It may be documented as:

- Positive
- Negative
- Unknown
- Not applicable

### Rechallenge

Rechallenge refers to the occurrence of the same or similar event after the suspected medicinal product is reintroduced.

Rechallenge information should be carefully documented when available.

---

# 11. Narrative Writing

A case narrative provides a concise chronological summary of the safety case.

A good narrative generally includes:

- Patient characteristics
- Relevant medical history
- Suspected medicinal product
- Indication
- Dose and administration
- Description of the adverse event
- Event onset
- Relevant investigations
- Action taken with the drug
- Treatment provided
- Dechallenge/rechallenge information
- Outcome
- Relevant additional information

### Narrative Writing Principles

- Follow a chronological order.
- Use clear and concise language.
- Maintain consistency with the source information.
- Do not introduce unsupported information.
- Clearly distinguish reported information from assessment.
- Maintain patient confidentiality.

---

# 12. Quality Control (QC)

After case processing, the case undergoes quality review.

The reviewer checks for:

- Completeness
- Accuracy
- Consistency
- Correct coding
- Correct seriousness assessment
- Correct expectedness assessment
- Appropriate causality assessment
- Narrative consistency
- Missing information
- Duplicate information
- Data discrepancies

Any identified errors or discrepancies should be corrected according to the applicable procedure.

---

# 13. Follow-up

If important information is missing, follow-up may be initiated with the reporter or appropriate source.

Examples of information that may require follow-up include:

- Additional patient information
- Event details
- Laboratory results
- Treatment information
- Outcome
- Dechallenge/rechallenge information
- Relevant medical history
- Concomitant medications
- Product details

Follow-up should be documented appropriately.

---

# 14. Case Submission

Cases that meet applicable reporting requirements are submitted to the relevant regulatory authority or other required recipients within the applicable reporting timelines.

Submission requirements may depend on:

- Seriousness
- Country/region
- Product type
- Source
- Regulatory requirements
- Reporting category

The case should be appropriately finalized before submission.

---

# 15. Case Closure and Archiving

After all required processing, assessment, quality checks, follow-up, and submission activities are completed, the case may be closed according to the applicable workflow.

Relevant case records and supporting information should be maintained securely for future reference, audits, inspections, or additional follow-up.

---

# 17. Importance of ICSR Processing

ICSR processing is important because it helps organizations:

- Identify potential safety concerns
- Monitor adverse drug reactions
- Maintain accurate safety databases
- Support signal detection
- Meet regulatory reporting requirements
- Protect patient safety
- Maintain the benefit-risk assessment of medicinal products
